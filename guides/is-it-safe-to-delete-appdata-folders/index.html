Is it safe to delete AppData folders?

Sometimes. AppData holds settings and data for the programs on your PC, and some of it is leftover from programs you removed. The trouble is telling which is which.

What AppData is

It is a hidden folder inside your user folder. It has three parts:

  • Local holds data for this PC, such as caches and temporary files.
  • LocalLow holds data for programs that run with lower permissions, such as some browsers' sandboxed content and games.
  • Roaming holds settings that can follow your account between PCs.

What is usually safe

  • The Windows Temp folder, once you close your programs. Skip files that say they are in use.
  • A folder named after a program you are sure you uninstalled and no longer want.

What to leave alone

  • Anything from Windows or a driver, such as Microsoft, NVIDIA or Intel folders.
  • Folders for programs you still use. Deleting them can reset your settings, saved games or logins.
  • A folder you cannot name. If you do not know it, do not delete it.

A safer way to clear one

  1. Search the folder name online and check it against your installed apps in Settings.
  2. Close the program if it is running.
  3. Move the folder to your desktop instead of deleting it.
  4. Use the PC for a few days. If nothing breaks, delete it then.

Remember that a folder with a program's name does not always mean the program is gone. Some apps install under a different name than the one in your Start menu.
