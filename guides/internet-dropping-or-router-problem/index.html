Internet dropping: your router or your provider?

When the internet cuts out, three quick tests show which part of the chain broke. You only need a Windows PC and about five minutes.

Run the tests while it is down

Open Command Prompt (press the Windows key, type cmd, press Enter). Run ipconfig and note the number next to "Default Gateway". That is your router. Then run these one at a time:

  1. ping followed by your router's number. This tests your PC to the router.
  2. ping 1.1.1.1. This tests your connection out to the internet.
  3. ping google.com. This tests name lookup (DNS).

What the results usually mean

  • Router test fails. The problem is between your PC and the router: Wi-Fi signal, a loose or bad Ethernet cable, or the router itself.
  • Router works, 1.1.1.1 fails. Your home network is fine and the connection beyond it is down. That points to your provider or the line to your house.
  • 1.1.1.1 works, google.com fails. Your internet is up but name lookup is broken. Try a different DNS server, or restart the router.
  • All three work but pages are slow. Look for high or jumping reply times. That suggests congestion or a weak signal, not a full outage.

These results are a strong hint, not proof. Some routers do not answer pings, and one test can be wrong.

Narrow it down

  • Try a wired connection if you normally use Wi-Fi. If the drops stop, the problem is your Wi-Fi.
  • Check whether other devices drop at the same time. If they all do, your PC is not the cause.
  • Look at the lights on your modem and router when it happens, and write down what they show.

The hard part is catching it

Many drops last under a minute and happen when you are not looking. Testing by hand only works if you are at the keyboard. Keep a log of when each drop starts and how long it lasts. A log is also what a provider's support team takes seriously. See how to log outages for your provider.
